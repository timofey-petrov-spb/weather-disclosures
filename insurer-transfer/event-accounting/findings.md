# A thousandfold unit error on two correctly located cells

The frozen v8 and v9 readers produced byte-identical outputs for all three previously unseen insurer families. Each returned two automatic records from the selected tables. Neither amount was correct in USD. Across six reference facts the score was **0/6** for exact USD amount and storm, **0/6** for the matching financial assertion and owner, **0/6** with the required sign and **0/6** for complete records. Distinct precision was **0/2**. The four reference facts in the remaining two tables received no automatic proposal or review flag.

**PXRE.** Table 23 identifies gross and net incurred losses as of December 31, 2005. The reader correctly identified the event row and both financial columns. It even cited the heading `($000’s)` as currency evidence, but stored a scale of 1. It returned $771,010 and $638,010, exactly one thousandth of each reported loss. The table footnote explains that the net figure deducts outwards reinsurance recoveries. The $850.809m at the bottom covers three storms. [PXRE original filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1091748/000112528206001680/b412207_10k.htm)

**Converium.** The sentence directly before table 104 defines the measure as large net incurred property catastrophe losses. The first column names Katrina, the caption gives USD millions, and the row shows 44.6. The measure is supplied by the introduction rather than a column heading. A later Run-Off segment paragraph triggered an automatic $15.6m proposal. The table footnote and the paragraph both say that figure covers Katrina, Rita and Wilma combined. Another later paragraph explicitly corroborates the group’s $44.6m Katrina loss. [Converium original filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1162586/000115697306000763/u50380e20vf.htm)

**Philadelphia Consolidated.** In table 57, Katrina is a column. The net loss and loss adjustment expense estimate is a section heading, whereas the three row labels identify the two segments and their total. The combined evidence gives $2.0m for personal lines and $5.7m for commercial lines, adding to $7.7m. A preceding paragraph explains that Katrina counted as two commercial occurrences. The $5.0m contractual retention per commercial occurrence and the $24.7m four-storm total answer different questions. The filing produced zero proposals overall. [Philadelphia Consolidated original filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/909109/000089322006000532/w18434e10vk.htm)

**Outputs elsewhere in the filings.** Each version returned eight additional automatic proposals and no review-only rows. Four proposals were attributable to Katrina at the precision of the quoted prose. They represent three distinct PXRE assertions and one repeat: the rounded year-end gross loss of $771.0m, an earlier September estimate of $556.4m gross, and $638.0m of current-year net losses. Some structured fields omit the basis or valuation date given in the accompanying text. The other four proposals assigned combined storm figures to Katrina. The $806.9m PXRE proposal also confused a loss after recoveries and premium effects with the amount recovered. These eight proposals have been reviewed individually. The table reference does not cover every financial fact in each filing, so a whole-filing recall cannot be estimated.

**Scope.** The original selection screened nine insurer families and found one eligible table. Its two facts both had scale errors. The prespecified reserve supplied two more eligible families after its first three candidates were screened. Their four facts all lacked proposals. Both source readings agreed on all six displayed cells, their economic meaning and their USD values before predictions were generated. Every selected table comes from FY2005 and describes Katrina. All six values are positive, so the test supplies no estimate of performance on negative amounts. There is no exhaustive reference for non-target cells, so specificity is undefined. Six facts in three related historical settings cannot establish an industry-wide accuracy rate.
